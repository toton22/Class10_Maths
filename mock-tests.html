Mock Tests

Two original papers in the exact CBSE pattern — 80 marks, 38 questions, 3 hours — each with a model answer and a step-wise marking scheme for every question, so you can mark yourself the way an examiner would.

  • Mock Test 1 →Case studies on arithmetic progressions, coordinate geometry and heights & distances. Long answers: quadratic word problem, Basic Proportionality Theorem, a combined solid, grouped data.
  • Mock Test 2 →Case studies on statistics, a sector-shaped badge and similar triangles. Long answers: AP word problem, tangents from an external point, two angles of depression, a tent.

How to take a mock test so that it counts

  1. Finish the syllabus first. A mock test measures exam skill, not chapter knowledge. Use each chapter's Board practice tab while you are still learning.
  2. Print the paper with the “Print question paper” button (solutions are left out), or keep the page open with all solutions closed.
  3. Sit for three hours without a break, at a table, with only a pen, pencil, ruler and compass. No calculator, no phone, no formula sheet.
  4. Follow the time plan from the Exam Strategy page and leave 15 minutes to check.
  5. Mark yourself honestly against the marking scheme under each solution — give yourself a step mark only if that step is actually on your paper.
  6. Fill the blueprint table at the end of the test. It shows which chapters cost you marks; revise those from their Revise tab and redo the starred questions.

Take Mock Test 1 about four weeks before the exam and Mock Test 2 about ten days before. In between, solve the official CBSE sample paper and at least three previous-year papers from the Board Papers page under the same conditions.
