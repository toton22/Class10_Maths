Chapter 11 Areas Related to Circles

A slice of pizza, the sweep of a windscreen wiper, the grass a tethered horse can reach — every one of them is a part of a circle. One small fraction, θ⁄360, turns all of them into numbers.

    11.1 Areas of sector and segment of a circle

    Class IX gave you the whole circle: circumference \(2\pi r\) and area \(\pi r^2\). This chapter gives you a part of it — the slice between two radii, and the piece a chord cuts off. Everything here comes from one honest idea: work out what fraction of the circle you want, then take that fraction of the whole.

    From Class IX, for a circle of radius \(r\) (diameter \(d=2r\)):

    $$\text{circumference}=2\pi r=\pi d,\qquad \text{area}=\pi r^2$$

    Take \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\) unless the question says \(\pi=3.14\). A full turn at the centre is \(360^\circ\).

    The words first: sector, segment, arc, chord

    Two radii cut a circular region into two pieces. A chord cuts it into two pieces as well. Those pieces have names, and the whole chapter is written in them, so learn them now.

    Definition

    A sector of a circle is the part of the circular region enclosed by two radii and the arc between them. The angle between those two radii, measured at the centre, is called the angle of the sector.

    A segment of a circle is the part of the circular region enclosed by a chord and the arc it cuts off.

    Each of them comes in two sizes. In the pictures below, the two radii \(OA\) and \(OB\) cut the region into the small sector \(OAPB\) and the big sector \(OAQB\); we call them the minor sector and the major sector. In the same way the chord \(AB\) cuts the region into the minor segment \(APB\) and the major segment \(AQB\).

    The book's short-hand: when a question just says “the sector” or “the segment”, it means the minor one, unless it says otherwise. If the answer is supposed to be the big piece, the question will say major.

    Centre \(O\)
    the fixed point; every radius starts here
    Radius \(r\)
    centre to any point of the circle
    Chord
    a line segment joining two points of the circle
    Arc
    a piece of the circle itself (a curve, so its size is a length in cm, not an area)
    Sector
    region bounded by two radii and an arc — the centre is a corner of it
    Segment
    region bounded by a chord and an arc — the centre is not part of its boundary

    Sector = Slice, cut with two straight cuts from the centre, like a slice of pizza. Segment = cut with one straight string (a chord), like the top you slice off a round cake. The slice keeps the centre; the segment does not.

    The one idea: \(\dfrac{\theta}{360}\) is the fraction of the circle

    The two radii of a sector make an angle \(\theta\) at the centre. A complete turn is \(360^\circ\) and it sweeps the whole circle. So a turn of \(\theta\) sweeps exactly \(\dfrac{\theta}{360}\) of the circle — of its area, and of its boundary.

    Area of a sector

    Let the sector \(OAPB\) have angle \(\theta\) in a circle of radius \(r\). Find its area by the unitary method.

    1. An angle of \(360^\circ\) at the centre corresponds to the whole circle, of area \(\pi r^2\).
    2. So an angle of \(1^\circ\) corresponds to an area of \(\dfrac{\pi r^2}{360}\).
    3. So an angle of \(\theta\) corresponds to an area of \(\dfrac{\pi r^2}{360}\times\theta=\dfrac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2\).
    Area of a sector of angle θ
    $$\text{Area of the sector}=\frac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2$$

    \(r\) = radius, \(\theta\) = angle of the sector in degrees. The answer is an area, so the unit is cm², m², km² — squared.

    Major sector, way 1

    Take the whole circle and remove the minor sector:

    $$\pi r^2-\frac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2$$

    Major sector, way 2

    Its own angle is \(360^\circ-\theta\), so use the same formula:

    $$\frac{360-\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2$$

    Both give the same number. Use whichever is less arithmetic — and if you have time, use the other one as a check.

    Length of an arc

    The same unitary method works on the boundary. The whole circle (angle \(360^\circ\)) has length \(2\pi r\), so an angle of \(\theta\) gives an arc of length \(\dfrac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r\).

    Length of the arc of a sector of angle θ
    $$\ell=\frac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r$$

    This is a length, so the unit is cm, m, km — not squared. It is the curved part only.

    Perimeter of a sector is asked often and is not the arc alone. Walking round the sector you go along one radius, round the arc and back along the other radius:

    $$\text{Perimeter of a sector}=\ell+2r=\frac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r+2r$$

    Divide the two formulas and you get a neat link between them:

    $$\text{Area of the sector}=\frac12\times\ell\times r$$

    because \(\tfrac12\times\bigl(\tfrac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r\bigr)\times r=\tfrac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2\). If a question gives you the arc length and the radius, this is the fastest route to the area.

    NCERT Example 1minor and major sector

    A circle has radius 4 cm. Find the area of a sector of this circle whose angle is \(30^\circ\), and also the area of the corresponding major sector. (Take \(\pi=3.14\).)

    Solution

    Given: \(r=4\) cm, \(\theta=30^\circ\), \(\pi=3.14\). To find: area of the minor sector and of the major sector.

    Minor sector \(OAPB\):

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{Area}&=\frac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2\\ &=\frac{30}{360}\times3.14\times4\times4\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{12.56}{3}\ \text{cm}^2=4.19\ \text{cm}^2\ \text{(approx.)} \end{aligned}$$

    Major sector, by taking the minor sector away from the whole circle:

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{Area}&=\pi r^2-4.19\\ &=(3.14\times16-4.19)\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=(50.24-4.19)\ \text{cm}^2=46.05\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    The same answer the other way. The angle of the major sector is \(360^\circ-30^\circ=330^\circ\), so

    $$\frac{330}{360}\times3.14\times16\ \text{cm}^2=46.05\ \text{cm}^2$$

    minor sector ≈ 4.19 cm², major sector ≈ 46.1 cm²

    \(12.56\div3=4.1866\ldots\), which is 4.19 correct to two decimal places; that is why the book writes “approx.” Keep the unrounded value in your calculator until the last step.

    Area of a segment

    Look at the minor segment \(APB\) and at the minor sector \(OAPB\). They differ by exactly one thing: the triangle \(OAB\). Put the triangle back on the segment and you get the sector. So take the triangle away from the sector and you get the segment.

    Area of a segment
    $$\text{Area of the segment } APB=\frac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2-\text{area of }\triangle OAB$$

    \(\theta\) is the angle the chord subtends at the centre. Do the two areas separately, then subtract — that is also how the marks are given.

    Major segment, way 1

    Whole circle minus the minor segment:

    $$\pi r^2-\text{area of segment }APB$$

    Major segment, way 2

    Major sector plus the triangle (the triangle is on the major side of the chord):

    $$\frac{360-\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2+\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)$$

    Two traps students fall into every year:

    1. Subtracting the triangle from the major sector. The triangle belongs to the minor sector, so it is subtracted there and added on the major side.

    2. Writing the arc length where the area was asked. Check the unit you are about to write: cm means \(2\pi r\), cm² means \(\pi r^2\).

    Finding the triangle — the only part that changes

    In \(\triangle OAB\) the two sides \(OA\) and \(OB\) are radii, so they are equal: the triangle is always isosceles, with the angle \(\theta\) between the equal sides. Three angles cover almost every question the board asks.

    \(\theta=60^\circ\)

    \(OA=OB\) and \(\angle O=60^\circ\), so the other two angles are \(60^\circ\) each: \(\triangle OAB\) is equilateral of side \(r\).

    $$\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}r^2$$

    \(\theta=90^\circ\)

    Right-angled at \(O\), with the two perpendicular sides equal to \(r\).

    $$\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)=\frac12\times r\times r=\frac12 r^2$$

    \(\theta=120^\circ\)

    Drop \(OM\perp AB\). Then \(OM=\tfrac r2\), \(AM=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}r\), so \(AB=\sqrt3\,r\).

    $$\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)=\frac12\times\sqrt3\,r\times\frac r2=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}r^2$$

    The \(120^\circ\) working is the general method, so learn it as a method and not as a result.

    1. Draw \(OM\perp AB\), with \(M\) on \(AB\).
    2. In \(\triangle AMO\) and \(\triangle BMO\): \(OA=OB\) (radii), \(OM=OM\) (common), \(\angle OMA=\angle OMB=90^\circ\). So \(\triangle AMO\cong\triangle BMO\) [RHS].
    3. Hence \(AM=MB\) (so \(M\) is the mid-point of \(AB\)) and \(\angle AOM=\angle BOM=\dfrac{\theta}{2}\).
    4. From right \(\triangle OMA\): \(OM=r\cos\dfrac{\theta}{2}\) and \(AM=r\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}\), so \(AB=2AM\).
    5. Finally \(\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)=\dfrac12\times AB\times OM\).

    Putting steps 4 and 5 together, for any angle \(\theta\),

    $$\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)=\frac12r^2\sin\theta$$

    Test it: \(\theta=60^\circ\) gives \(\tfrac12r^2\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}r^2\) ✓, \(\theta=90^\circ\) gives \(\tfrac12r^2\) ✓, and \(\theta=120^\circ\) gives \(\tfrac12r^2\cdot\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=\tfrac{\sqrt3}{4}r^2\) ✓ — the same as \(60^\circ\), because \(\sin120^\circ=\sin60^\circ\). Write the full \(OM\perp AB\) working in the exam and keep this line for checking.

    NCERT Example 2area of a segment

    In a circle with centre \(O\) and radius 21 cm, a chord \(AB\) subtends an angle of \(120^\circ\) at the centre. \(Y\) is a point of the minor arc \(AB\). Find the area of the segment \(AYB\). (Take \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\).)

    Solution

    Given: \(r=21\) cm, \(\angle AOB=120^\circ\), \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\). To find: area of the segment \(AYB\).

    Step 1 — the plan.

    $$\text{ar(segment }AYB)=\text{ar(sector }OAYB)-\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)\qquad\ldots(1)$$

    Step 2 — the sector.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(sector }OAYB)&=\frac{120}{360}\times\frac{22}{7}\times21\times21\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac13\times22\times63\ \text{cm}^2=462\ \text{cm}^2\qquad\ldots(2) \end{aligned}$$

    Step 3 — the triangle. Draw \(OM\perp AB\).

    \(OA=OB\) (radii), so by RHS congruence \(\triangle AMO\cong\triangle BMO\). Hence \(M\) is the mid-point of \(AB\) and

    $$\angle AOM=\angle BOM=\frac12\times120^\circ=60^\circ$$

    Let \(OM=x\) cm. From right \(\triangle OMA\),

    $$\begin{aligned} \frac{OM}{OA}=\cos60^\circ&\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{x}{21}=\frac12\ \Rightarrow\ x=\frac{21}{2}\\ \frac{AM}{OA}=\sin60^\circ&\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{AM}{21}=\frac{\sqrt3}{2}\ \Rightarrow\ AM=\frac{21\sqrt3}{2} \end{aligned}$$

    So \(AB=2\,AM=21\sqrt3\) cm, and

    $$\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)=\frac12\times AB\times OM=\frac12\times21\sqrt3\times\frac{21}{2}=\frac{441\sqrt3}{4}\ \text{cm}^2\qquad\ldots(3)$$

    Step 4 — subtract. From (1), (2) and (3),

    $$\text{ar(segment }AYB)=\left(462-\frac{441\sqrt3}{4}\right)\text{cm}^2=\frac{21}{4}\bigl(88-21\sqrt3\bigr)\ \text{cm}^2$$

    \(\dfrac{21}{4}\left(88-21\sqrt3\right)\) cm², about 271.04 cm²

    Taking \(\dfrac{21}{4}\) common is worth doing: \(\dfrac{21}{4}\times88=462\) and \(\dfrac{21}{4}\times21\sqrt3=\dfrac{441\sqrt3}{4}\). Unless the question gives a value of \(\sqrt3\), leave the surd in the answer.

    How this chapter is asked. One mark: name a part of a circle, or the fraction \(\theta/360\), or a direct substitution. Two marks: area of a sector or length of an arc. Three marks: area of a segment, or a word problem (wiper, clock, horse). Five marks or a case study: two sectors compared, or a design made of segments with a cost.

    How the three marks are split: 1 for writing the correct formula, 1 for the correct substitution, 1 for the final answer with its unit. You get the first mark even if the arithmetic goes wrong — so always write the formula first.

    Use the \(\pi\) the question wants, and cancel early. If the question prints \(\pi=3.14\), use 3.14 and expect a decimal answer; otherwise use \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\). Never mix the two inside one question.

    With \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\), look for a 7 to cancel: when the radius or the diameter is a multiple of 7 — 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, … — the answer comes out a whole number. When it is not (radius 45 in the umbrella question, 25 in the wiper question), the answer is meant to stay a fraction, so do not force it into a decimal.

    Degrees for minutes. A clock face is \(360^\circ\) for 60 minutes, so 1 minute = 6° for the minute hand (and 1 hour = 30° for the hour hand). Students write \(\theta=5\) instead of \(\theta=30^\circ\) for five minutes and lose the whole question.

    A sector of a circle of radius 14 cm has angle \(45^\circ\). What fraction of the circle is it, and what is its area? (Use \(\pi=\tfrac{22}{7}\).)

    Show answer

    \(\dfrac{45}{360}=\dfrac18\) of the circle. Area \(=\dfrac18\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times14\times14=\dfrac18\times616=77\) cm².

    In a circle of radius 10 cm a chord subtends \(90^\circ\) at the centre. Write the area of the minor segment in terms of \(\pi\).

    Show answer

    Sector \(=\dfrac{90}{360}\times\pi\times100=25\pi\) cm². Triangle \(=\dfrac12\times10\times10=50\) cm². Segment \(=(25\pi-50)\) cm².

    11.2 Summary

    Three results carry the whole chapter. Everything else is arithmetic and reading the question carefully.

    1. Length of an arcFor a sector of angle \(\theta\) in a circle of radius \(r\), the arc is \(\dfrac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r\).
    2. Area of a sectorFor the same sector of angle \(\theta\) in a circle of radius \(r\), the area is \(\dfrac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2\).
    3. Area of a segmentTake the area of the corresponding sector and subtract the area of the corresponding triangle.

    Which formula, and which fraction

    Almost every question in Exercise 11.1 is one line of the table below. Read the question, find the angle, and the rest is substitution.

    The question saysAngle \(\theta\)What to use
    quadrant\(90^\circ\)\(\tfrac14\) of the circle
    semicircle\(180^\circ\)\(\tfrac12\) of the circle
    minute hand, \(t\) minutes\(6t\)sector, radius = length of the hand
    \(n\) ribs / \(n\) equal sectors\(\dfrac{360}{n}\)\(\tfrac1n\) of the circle
    tied at the corner of a square field\(90^\circ\)quarter circle, radius = length of the rope
    chord subtends \(\theta\)\(\theta\)segment = sector − triangle
    regular hexagon inside a circle\(60^\circ\)six equal segments; side of hexagon = \(r\)

    • Units

      Length in cm, m, km. Area in cm², m², km². Cost = area × rate, so check that the rate's unit matches your area's unit.

    • Two values of π

      \(\dfrac{22}{7}\) unless the question prints 3.14. Use \(\sqrt3=1.73\) (or 1.7) only when the question gives it.

    • Exact or decimal?

      If no value of \(\sqrt3\) is given, leave \(\sqrt3\) in the answer. If no value of \(\pi\) is given either, leave \(\pi\) in it.

    A wheel of radius 35 cm turns through \(72^\circ\). How far does a point of its rim travel? (Use \(\pi=\tfrac{22}{7}\).)

    Show answer

    This is an arc length: \(\dfrac{72}{360}\times2\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times35=\dfrac15\times220=44\) cm.

    Exercise 11.1

    Exercise 11.1

    14 questions · area of a sector, length of an arc, area of a segment, and the standard word problems

    Unless a question says otherwise, take \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\). Where a question prints \(\pi=3.14\) or a value of \(\sqrt3\), use exactly that value — the official answers are worked out with it.

    Q1

    A circle has radius 6 cm. Find the area of a sector of this circle whose angle is \(60^\circ\).

    Solution

    Given: \(r=6\) cm, \(\theta=60^\circ\), \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\). To find: area of the sector.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{Area of the sector}&=\frac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2\\ &=\frac{60}{360}\times\frac{22}{7}\times6\times6\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac16\times\frac{22}{7}\times36\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{22\times6}{7}\ \text{cm}^2=\frac{132}{7}\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    \(\dfrac{132}{7}\) cm² \(=18\dfrac67\) cm² ≈ 18.86 cm²

    \(60^\circ\) is one sixth of \(360^\circ\), so this sector is one sixth of the circle: \(\tfrac16\times\tfrac{22}{7}\times36\). Checking the fraction first is a quick way to catch a slip.

    Q2

    The circumference of a circle is 22 cm. Find the area of a quadrant of this circle.

    Solution

    Given: circumference \(=22\) cm. To find: area of a quadrant (a sector of angle \(90^\circ\)).

    Step 1 — find the radius from the circumference.

    $$\begin{aligned} 2\pi r&=22\\ 2\times\frac{22}{7}\times r&=22\\ \frac{44r}{7}&=22\\ r&=\frac{22\times7}{44}=\frac72\ \text{cm} \end{aligned}$$

    Step 2 — area of the quadrant. A quadrant has \(\theta=90^\circ\), so it is \(\dfrac{90}{360}=\dfrac14\) of the circle.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{Area}&=\frac14\times\pi r^2=\frac14\times\frac{22}{7}\times\frac72\times\frac72\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{22\times7}{16}\ \text{cm}^2=\frac{154}{16}\ \text{cm}^2=\frac{77}{8}\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    \(\dfrac{77}{8}\) cm² \(=9.625\) cm²

    Do not put \(r=22\). The 22 cm is the distance round the circle, so you must first undo \(2\pi r\) to get \(r=3.5\) cm.

    Q3

    The minute hand of a clock is 14 cm long. Find the area it sweeps in 5 minutes.

    Solution

    Given: length of the minute hand \(r=14\) cm, time = 5 minutes. To find: the area swept.

    Step 1 — turn the minutes into degrees. The minute hand makes one complete turn, \(360^\circ\), in 60 minutes. So in 1 minute it turns

    $$\frac{360^\circ}{60}=6^\circ,\qquad\text{and in 5 minutes}\quad \theta=5\times6^\circ=30^\circ$$

    Step 2 — the swept region is a sector of radius 14 cm and angle \(30^\circ\).

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{Area swept}&=\frac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2\\ &=\frac{30}{360}\times\frac{22}{7}\times14\times14\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac1{12}\times22\times28\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{616}{12}\ \text{cm}^2=\frac{154}{3}\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    \(\dfrac{154}{3}\) cm² \(=51\dfrac13\) cm² ≈ 51.33 cm²

    The hand sweeps a sector, not an arc: the whole triangle-shaped fan is covered, so the answer is an area in cm².

    Q4

    A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends a right angle at the centre. Find the area of the corresponding

    1. minor segment;
    2. major sector. (Use \(\pi=3.14\))
    Solution

    Given: \(r=10\) cm, \(\angle AOB=\theta=90^\circ\), \(\pi=3.14\). To find: (i) area of the minor segment, (ii) area of the major sector.

    First, the minor sector \(OAB\):

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(sector }OAB)&=\frac{90}{360}\times3.14\times10\times10\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac14\times314\ \text{cm}^2=78.5\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Next, \(\triangle OAB\). It is right-angled at \(O\), and its two perpendicular sides are the radii \(OA=OB=10\) cm.

    $$\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)=\frac12\times10\times10=50\ \text{cm}^2$$
    (i)
    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(minor segment)}&=\text{ar(sector }OAB)-\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)\\ &=(78.5-50)\ \text{cm}^2=28.5\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$
    (ii)

    The angle of the major sector is \(360^\circ-90^\circ=270^\circ\).

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(major sector)}&=\frac{270}{360}\times3.14\times100\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac34\times314\ \text{cm}^2=235.5\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Check: area of the circle \(-\) minor sector \(=314-78.5=235.5\) cm². ✓

    (i) 28.5 cm²   (ii) 235.5 cm²

    Part (i) asks for a segment (so subtract the triangle) and part (ii) for a sector (so do not). Reading those two words correctly is where the marks are.

    Q5

    In a circle of radius 21 cm, an arc subtends an angle of \(60^\circ\) at the centre. Find

    1. the length of the arc;
    2. the area of the sector formed by this arc;
    3. the area of the segment cut off by the corresponding chord.
    Solution

    Given: \(r=21\) cm, \(\theta=60^\circ\), \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\).

    (i)
    $$\begin{aligned} \text{Length of the arc}&=\frac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r\\ &=\frac{60}{360}\times2\times\frac{22}{7}\times21\ \text{cm}\\ &=\frac16\times2\times22\times3\ \text{cm}=\frac{132}{6}\ \text{cm}=22\ \text{cm} \end{aligned}$$
    (ii)
    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(sector }OAPB)&=\frac{60}{360}\times\frac{22}{7}\times21\times21\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac16\times22\times63\ \text{cm}^2=\frac{1386}{6}\ \text{cm}^2=231\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Check with \(\text{area}=\tfrac12\ell r=\tfrac12\times22\times21=231\) cm². ✓

    (iii)

    In \(\triangle OAB\), \(OA=OB=21\) cm (radii) and \(\angle AOB=60^\circ\). The other two angles are therefore \(60^\circ\) each, so \(\triangle OAB\) is equilateral with side 21 cm.

    $$\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\times21^2=\frac{441\sqrt3}{4}\ \text{cm}^2$$ $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(segment)}&=\text{ar(sector)}-\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)\\ &=\left(231-\frac{441\sqrt3}{4}\right)\text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    (i) 22 cm   (ii) 231 cm²   (iii) \(\left(231-\dfrac{441\sqrt3}{4}\right)\) cm² ≈ 40.04 cm²

    No value of \(\sqrt3\) is given, so the exact surd form is the required answer; the decimal is only for your own sense of size.

    Q6

    A chord of a circle of radius 15 cm subtends an angle of \(60^\circ\) at the centre. Find the areas of the corresponding minor and major segments of the circle. (Use \(\pi=3.14\) and \(\sqrt3=1.73\))

    Solution

    Given: \(r=15\) cm, \(\theta=60^\circ\), \(\pi=3.14\), \(\sqrt3=1.73\). To find: areas of the minor and major segments.

    Step 1 — the minor sector \(OAB\).

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(sector }OAB)&=\frac{60}{360}\times3.14\times15\times15\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac16\times3.14\times225\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{706.5}{6}\ \text{cm}^2=117.75\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Step 2 — \(\triangle OAB\). \(OA=OB=15\) cm (radii) and \(\angle AOB=60^\circ\), so the remaining two angles are \(60^\circ\) each and the triangle is equilateral with side 15 cm.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar}(\triangle OAB)&=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\times15^2=\frac{1.73}{4}\times225\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{389.25}{4}\ \text{cm}^2=97.3125\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Step 3 — the minor segment.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(minor segment)}&=117.75-97.3125\\ &=20.4375\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Step 4 — the major segment is the rest of the circle.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(circle)}&=3.14\times15\times15=706.5\ \text{cm}^2\\ \text{ar(major segment)}&=706.5-20.4375\\ &=686.0625\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    minor segment 20.4375 cm², major segment 686.0625 cm²

    Second route to the major segment: major sector \(+\ \triangle OAB=\dfrac{300}{360}\times706.5+97.3125=588.75+97.3125=686.0625\) cm². The triangle lies on the major side of the chord, so here it is added.

    Q7

    A chord of a circle of radius 12 cm subtends an angle of \(120^\circ\) at the centre. Find the area of the corresponding segment of the circle. (Use \(\pi=3.14\) and \(\sqrt3=1.73\))

    Solution

    Given: \(r=12\) cm, \(\theta=120^\circ\), \(\pi=3.14\), \(\sqrt3=1.73\). To find: area of the minor segment.

    Step 1 — the sector \(OAB\).

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(sector }OAB)&=\frac{120}{360}\times3.14\times12\times12\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac13\times3.14\times144\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{452.16}{3}\ \text{cm}^2=150.72\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Step 2 — \(\triangle OAB\). Draw \(OM\perp AB\). In \(\triangle AMO\) and \(\triangle BMO\): \(OA=OB\) (radii), \(OM=OM\) (common) and \(\angle OMA=\angle OMB=90^\circ\), so \(\triangle AMO\cong\triangle BMO\) [RHS]. Hence \(M\) is the mid-point of \(AB\) and

    $$\angle AOM=\angle BOM=\frac12\times120^\circ=60^\circ$$

    From right \(\triangle OMA\),

    $$\begin{aligned} OM&=OA\cos60^\circ=12\times\frac12=6\ \text{cm}\\ AM&=OA\sin60^\circ=12\times\frac{\sqrt3}{2}=6\sqrt3\ \text{cm} \end{aligned}$$

    So \(AB=2\,AM=12\sqrt3\) cm, and

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar}(\triangle OAB)&=\frac12\times AB\times OM=\frac12\times12\sqrt3\times6\\ &=36\sqrt3=36\times1.73=62.28\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Step 3 — the segment.

    $$\text{ar(segment)}=150.72-62.28=88.44\ \text{cm}^2$$

    88.44 cm²

    Quick check with the shortcut \(\tfrac12r^2\sin\theta=\tfrac12\times144\times\sin120^\circ=72\times\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}=36\sqrt3\) — the same triangle. In the exam still write the \(OM\perp AB\) working; that is what carries the marks.

    Q8

    A horse is tied to a peg at one corner of a square grass field of side 15 m, by a rope 5 m long. Find

    1. the area of the part of the field the horse can graze;
    2. the increase in the grazing area if the rope is made 10 m long instead of 5 m. (Use \(\pi=3.14\))
    Solution

    Given: square field \(ABCD\) of side 15 m, peg at the corner \(A\), \(\pi=3.14\). To find: (i) grazed area with a 5 m rope, (ii) the increase when the rope becomes 10 m.

    Each angle of a square is \(90^\circ\), so the rope of length \(r\) sweeps a quarter circle of radius \(r\) with centre \(A\), lying inside the field (because \(r\lt15\) m in both cases).

    (i)

    Rope \(r=5\) m, \(\theta=90^\circ\):

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(grazed)}&=\frac{90}{360}\times\pi r^2=\frac14\times3.14\times5\times5\ \text{m}^2\\ &=\frac{78.5}{4}\ \text{m}^2=19.625\ \text{m}^2 \end{aligned}$$
    (ii)

    Rope \(r=10\) m, \(\theta=90^\circ\):

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(new grazed)}&=\frac14\times3.14\times10\times10\ \text{m}^2\\ &=\frac{314}{4}\ \text{m}^2=78.5\ \text{m}^2 \end{aligned}$$ $$\begin{aligned} \text{Increase}&=78.5-19.625\\ &=58.875\ \text{m}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    (i) 19.625 m²   (ii) increase = 58.875 m²

    The official answer key prints the increase as “58.875 cm²”. That is a slip in the unit only — the field and the rope are measured in metres, so the area must be in m². The number 58.875 is correct.

    Part (ii) asks for the increase, not the new area. Doubling the rope does not double the area: it multiplies it by \(2^2=4\), so the extra grass is three times the original patch — \(3\times19.625=58.875\) m². ✓

    Q9

    A brooch is made of silver wire in the shape of a circle of diameter 35 mm. More of the same wire is used to make 5 diameters, which cut the circle into 10 equal sectors. Find

    1. the total length of silver wire needed;
    2. the area of each sector of the brooch.
    Solution

    Given: diameter \(d=35\) mm, so radius \(r=\dfrac{35}{2}\) mm; 5 diameters; 10 equal sectors; \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\).

    (i)

    The wire makes the rim (one full circumference) and 5 straight diameters.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{circumference}&=\pi d=\frac{22}{7}\times35=110\ \text{mm}\\ \text{5 diameters}&=5\times35=175\ \text{mm}\\ \text{total length}&=110+175=285\ \text{mm} \end{aligned}$$
    (ii)

    The 10 sectors are equal, so each has angle

    $$\theta=\frac{360^\circ}{10}=36^\circ$$ $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(each sector)}&=\frac{36}{360}\times\pi r^2=\frac1{10}\times\frac{22}{7}\times\frac{35}{2}\times\frac{35}{2}\ \text{mm}^2\\ &=\frac1{10}\times\frac{22\times175}{4}\ \text{mm}^2\\ &=\frac{3850}{40}\ \text{mm}^2=\frac{385}{4}\ \text{mm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    (i) 285 mm   (ii) \(\dfrac{385}{4}\) mm² \(=96.25\) mm²

    Five diameters give ten sectors, not five — each diameter is cut into two radii at the centre. You can also get (ii) as \(\tfrac1{10}\times\pi r^2=\tfrac1{10}\times962.5=96.25\) mm².

    Q10

    An umbrella has 8 equally spaced ribs. Treating the open umbrella as a flat circle of radius 45 cm, find the area of the cloth between two consecutive ribs.

    Solution

    Given: \(r=45\) cm, 8 equally spaced ribs, \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\). To find: the area between two consecutive ribs.

    The 8 ribs are equally spaced, so they cut the circle into 8 equal sectors and

    $$\theta=\frac{360^\circ}{8}=45^\circ$$ $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(between two ribs)}&=\frac{45}{360}\times\frac{22}{7}\times45\times45\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac18\times\frac{22}{7}\times2025\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{44550}{56}\ \text{cm}^2=\frac{22275}{28}\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    \(\dfrac{22275}{28}\) cm² \(=795\dfrac{15}{28}\) cm² ≈ 795.54 cm²

    Same thing in one line: the area asked for is \(\dfrac18\) of the whole circle, \(\dfrac18\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times2025\). Leave the answer as an improper fraction — that is how the NCERT key gives it.

    Q11

    A car has two wipers whose swept regions do not overlap. Each wiper blade is 25 cm long and turns through an angle of \(115^\circ\). Find the total area cleaned in one sweep of the blades.

    Solution

    Given: 2 wipers, blade length \(r=25\) cm, \(\theta=115^\circ\) each, no overlap, \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\). To find: the total area cleaned in one sweep.

    Step 1 — area cleaned by one blade (a sector of radius 25 cm and angle \(115^\circ\)):

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(one sector)}&=\frac{115}{360}\times\frac{22}{7}\times25\times25\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{23}{72}\times\frac{22}{7}\times625\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{23\times22\times625}{72\times7}\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{316250}{504}\ \text{cm}^2=\frac{158125}{252}\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Step 2 — two blades. The two swept regions do not overlap, so no part is counted twice and the total is simply twice as much.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{Total area cleaned}&=2\times\frac{158125}{252}\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{158125}{126}\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    \(\dfrac{158125}{126}\) cm² ≈ 1254.96 cm²

    “Do not overlap” is the key phrase: it lets you add. If the sectors overlapped you would have to subtract the common part once.

    Q12

    To warn ships about underwater rocks, a lighthouse throws red light over a sector of angle \(80^\circ\), reaching a distance of 16.5 km. Find the area of sea over which the ships are warned. (Use \(\pi=3.14\))

    Solution

    Given: \(r=16.5\) km, \(\theta=80^\circ\), \(\pi=3.14\). To find: the area of the warned sector.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{Area}&=\frac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2\\ &=\frac{80}{360}\times3.14\times16.5\times16.5\ \text{km}^2\\ &=\frac29\times3.14\times272.25\ \text{km}^2\\ &=\frac{2\times854.865}{9}\ \text{km}^2\\ &=\frac{1709.73}{9}\ \text{km}^2=189.97\ \text{km}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Q13

    A round table cover of radius 28 cm has six equal designs on it, as shown. Each design is the piece between a side of the regular hexagon drawn inside the cover and the arc beyond it. Find the cost of making the designs at ₹ 0.35 per cm². (Use \(\sqrt3=1.7\))

    Solution

    Given: \(r=28\) cm, 6 equal designs, rate ₹ 0.35 per cm², \(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\), \(\sqrt3=1.7\). To find: the total cost.

    Step 1 — the angle of one design. The six chords divide the whole angle at the centre into six equal parts, so for one design

    $$\theta=\frac{360^\circ}{6}=60^\circ$$

    Each design is therefore the segment cut off by a chord that subtends \(60^\circ\) at the centre.

    Step 2 — the sector of \(60^\circ\).

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(sector }OAB)&=\frac{60}{360}\times\frac{22}{7}\times28\times28\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac16\times22\times112\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=\frac{2464}{6}\ \text{cm}^2=\frac{1232}{3}\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Step 3 — the triangle. \(OA=OB=28\) cm and \(\angle AOB=60^\circ\), so \(\triangle OAB\) is equilateral with side 28 cm.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar}(\triangle OAB)&=\frac{\sqrt3}{4}\times28^2=\frac{1.7}{4}\times784\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=1.7\times196\ \text{cm}^2=333.2\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Step 4 — one design, then all six.

    $$\begin{aligned} \text{ar(one design)}&=\frac{1232}{3}-333.2\ \text{cm}^2\\ \text{ar(six designs)}&=6\times\frac{1232}{3}-6\times333.2\ \text{cm}^2\\ &=2464-1999.2\ \text{cm}^2=464.8\ \text{cm}^2 \end{aligned}$$

    Step 5 — the cost.

    $$\text{Cost}=464.8\times0.35=162.68$$

    total area of the designs = 464.8 cm², cost = ₹ 162.68

    Multiply by 6 before rounding. One design is \(\dfrac{1232}{3}-333.2=77.46\overline{6}\) cm²; rounding that to 77.47 first would push the cost to ₹ 162.69.

    Q14

    Tick the correct answer. The area of a sector of angle \(p\) (in degrees) of a circle of radius \(R\) is

    1. \(\dfrac{p}{180}\times2\pi R\)
    2. \(\dfrac{p}{180}\times\pi R^2\)
    3. \(\dfrac{p}{360}\times2\pi R\)
    4. \(\dfrac{p}{720}\times2\pi R^2\)
    Solution

    The angle of the sector is \(p\) and a full turn is \(360^\circ\), so the sector is \(\dfrac{p}{360}\) of the circle, whose area is \(\pi R^2\):

    $$\text{ar(sector)}=\frac{p}{360}\times\pi R^2$$

    That expression is not printed as it stands, so rewrite it. Multiplying the numerator and the denominator by 2,

    $$\frac{p}{360}\times\pi R^2=\frac{2p}{720}\times\pi R^2=\frac{p}{720}\times2\pi R^2$$

    which is the fourth option.

    Why the others are wrong:

    1. \(\dfrac{p}{180}\times2\pi R\) has only one power of \(R\), so it is a length, not an area.
    2. \(\dfrac{p}{180}\times\pi R^2\) is twice the correct area — 180 has been used where 360 belongs.
    3. \(\dfrac{p}{360}\times2\pi R\) is the length of the arc of this sector, not its area.

    (d) \(\dfrac{p}{720}\times2\pi R^2\)

    The NCERT book labels the four choices (A), (B), (C), (D) and the answer key gives D — the same fourth option shown here as (d).

    A triangular grassy field \(ABC\) has sides 30 m, 40 m and 50 m, and its area is 600 m\(^2\). A calf is tied at each of the three corners by a rope 7 m long, and the ropes are short enough for the grazed regions not to overlap. Find

    1. the total area grazed by the three calves,
    2. the area of the field left ungrazed,
    3. the increase in the total grazed area if each rope is lengthened to 14 m.
    Answer & solution

    Given: \(\text{ar}(\triangle ABC)=600\ \text{m}^2\); each rope \(r=7\) m.

    (i)

    The three grazed regions are sectors of the same radius \(r=7\) m with angles \(\angle A\), \(\angle B\), \(\angle C\), and \(\angle A+\angle B+\angle C=180^\circ\).

    $$\begin{aligned}\text{Total grazed}&=\frac{\angle A+\angle B+\angle C}{360}\times\pi r^2\\&=\frac{180}{360}\times\frac{22}{7}\times49\end{aligned}$$

    \(=\dfrac12\times154=77\ \text{m}^2\).

    (ii)

    Ungrazed area \(=600-77=523\ \text{m}^2\).

    (iii)

    With \(r=14\) m the sectors still do not overlap, because the shortest side of the field is 30 m and \(14+14=28\ \text{m}\lt30\ \text{m}\).

    Total grazed \(=\dfrac12\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times14\times14=\dfrac12\times616=308\ \text{m}^2\).

    Increase \(=308-77=231\ \text{m}^2\).

    77 m\(^2\); 523 m\(^2\); increase \(=231\ \text{m}^2\)

    The three sectors always join up into exactly a semicircle of radius \(r\) — you never need the individual angles.

    2 marks for (i) with the angle-sum reason · 1 mark for (ii) · 2 marks for (iii).

    The area of a sector of a circle is 231 cm\(^2\) and the length of its arc is 22 cm. Find

    1. the radius of the circle,
    2. the angle of the sector,
    3. the perimeter of the sector,
    4. the area of the corresponding major sector.
    Answer & solution
    (i)

    For any sector, area \(=\dfrac12\times\text{arc}\times\text{radius}\).

    $$\begin{aligned}231&=\frac12\times22\times r\\ 11r&=231\\ r&=21\text{ cm}\end{aligned}$$
    (ii)

    Circumference \(=2\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times21=132\) cm, so \(\dfrac{\theta}{360}=\dfrac{22}{132}=\dfrac16\) and \(\theta=60^\circ\).

    (iii)

    Perimeter \(=\) arc \(+\ 2r=22+2\times21=64\) cm.

    (iv)

    Area of the circle \(=\dfrac{22}{7}\times21\times21=1386\ \text{cm}^2\).

    Major sector \(=1386-231=1155\ \text{cm}^2\).

    Check: the major sector has angle \(360^\circ-60^\circ=300^\circ\), and \(\frac{300}{360}\times1386=1155\ \text{cm}^2\). ✓

    \(r=21\) cm, \(\theta=60^\circ\), perimeter \(=64\) cm, major sector \(=1155\ \text{cm}^2\)

    2 marks for the radius · 1 mark for the angle · 1 mark for the perimeter · 1 mark for the major sector.

    \(PQ\) is a chord of a circle with centre \(O\) and radius 14 cm, and \(\angle POQ=90^\circ\). Find

    1. the length of the minor arc \(PQ\),
    2. the area of the minor sector \(OPQ\),
    3. the area of the minor segment,
    4. the area of the major segment.

    ORFor the same circle and chord, find the perimeter of the minor sector \(OPQ\) and the perimeter of the minor segment. (Use \(\sqrt2=1.41\).)

    Answer & solution

    Given: \(r=14\) cm, \(\angle POQ=90^\circ\). Area of the circle \(=\dfrac{22}{7}\times14\times14=616\ \text{cm}^2\); circumference \(=88\) cm.

    (i)

    Minor arc \(PQ=\dfrac{90}{360}\times88=22\) cm.

    (ii)

    Minor sector \(=\dfrac{90}{360}\times616=154\ \text{cm}^2\).

    (iii)

    \(\triangle OPQ\) is right-angled at \(O\), so \(\text{ar}(\triangle OPQ)=\dfrac12\times14\times14=98\ \text{cm}^2\).

    Minor segment \(=154-98=56\ \text{cm}^2\).

    (iv)

    Major segment \(=616-56=560\ \text{cm}^2\).

    Check: major segment \(=\) major sector \(+\ \triangle OPQ=462+98=560\ \text{cm}^2\). ✓

    22 cm, 154 cm\(^2\), 56 cm\(^2\), 560 cm\(^2\)

    OR: The minor sector is bounded by the arc and the two radii, so its perimeter \(=22+14+14=50\) cm.

    The minor segment is bounded by the arc and the chord. By Pythagoras in \(\triangle OPQ\),

    $$\begin{aligned}PQ&=\sqrt{14^2+14^2}=14\sqrt2\\&=14\times1.41=19.74\text{ cm}\end{aligned}$$

    Perimeter of the minor segment \(=22+19.74=41.74\) cm.

    50 cm and 41.74 cm

    1 mark each for (i)–(iv), with 1 mark for stating the formulas used. OR: 2 marks for the sector, 3 for the segment.

    Long answer5 marks

    Sector \(A\) is cut from a circle of radius 28 cm and has an angle of \(45^\circ\). Sector \(B\) is a semicircle of radius 14 cm.

    1. Find the area of each sector, and say which is larger.
    2. Find the length of the arc of each sector.
    3. Find the perimeter of each sector, and say which is larger.
    Answer & solution
    (i)

    Sector \(A\): \(\dfrac{45}{360}\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times28\times28=\dfrac18\times2464=308\ \text{cm}^2\).

    Sector \(B\): \(\dfrac{180}{360}\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times14\times14=\dfrac12\times616=308\ \text{cm}^2\).

    The two areas are equal; neither is larger.

    (ii)

    Arc of \(A=\dfrac{45}{360}\times2\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times28=\dfrac18\times176=22\) cm.

    Arc of \(B=\dfrac{180}{360}\times2\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times14=\dfrac12\times88=44\) cm.

    (iii)

    Perimeter of \(A=22+2\times28=78\) cm.

    Perimeter of \(B=44+2\times14=72\) cm.

    So \(A\) has the greater perimeter, by \(78-72=6\) cm.

    Both areas are 308 cm\(^2\); arcs 22 cm and 44 cm; perimeters 78 cm and 72 cm, so \(A\) has the larger perimeter.

    Equal areas do not mean equal perimeters. Halving the radius and multiplying the angle by 4 keeps \(\frac{\theta}{360}r^2\) the same, but changes \(\frac{\theta}{360}r\) — and so changes the arc.

    2 marks for the two areas · 1 mark for the two arcs · 2 marks for the perimeters and the comparison.

    The school wall clock. The clock in the school assembly hall has a minute hand 14 cm long and an hour hand 7 cm long. Each hand sweeps out a sector of a circle as it turns, and the school wants to know how much of the dial each hand covers.

    1. Through what angle does the minute hand turn in 20 minutes? 1
    2. Find the area swept by the minute hand in 15 minutes. 1
    3. Find the area swept by the hour hand between 9 a.m. and 3 p.m. 2ORFind the distance moved by the tip of the minute hand in 45 minutes.
    Answer & solution
    (i)

    The minute hand completes \(360^\circ\) in 60 minutes, so it turns \(6^\circ\) per minute.

    In 20 minutes it turns \(20\times6^\circ=120^\circ\).

    (ii)

    In 15 minutes the angle is \(15\times6^\circ=90^\circ\), and \(r=14\) cm.

    Area \(=\dfrac{90}{360}\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times14\times14=\dfrac14\times616=154\ \text{cm}^2\).

    (iii)

    The hour hand completes \(360^\circ\) in 12 hours, so it turns \(30^\circ\) per hour. From 9 a.m. to 3 p.m. is 6 hours, an angle of \(6\times30^\circ=180^\circ\). Here \(r=7\) cm.

    Area \(=\dfrac{180}{360}\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times7\times7=\dfrac12\times154=77\ \text{cm}^2\).

    OR: In 45 minutes the minute hand turns \(45\times6^\circ=270^\circ\). Its tip moves along an arc of the circle of radius 14 cm:

    Distance \(=\dfrac{270}{360}\times2\times\dfrac{22}{7}\times14=\dfrac34\times88=66\) cm.

    (i) \(120^\circ\) (ii) 154 cm\(^2\) (iii) 77 cm\(^2\); OR 66 cm

    Watch the radius: the minute hand and the hour hand have different lengths, and they turn at different rates (\(6^\circ\) and \(0.5^\circ\) per minute).

    Areas Related to Circles

    Sector

    • Area \(=\dfrac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2\)
    • Arc \(=\dfrac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r\)
    • Perimeter \(=\) arc \(+\,2r\) (never forget the two radii)
    • Major sector: use \(360^\circ-\theta\)

    Segment

    • Segment \(=\) sector \(-\ \triangle OAB\)
    • Major segment \(=\) circle \(-\) minor segment
    • Also: major segment \(=\) major sector \(+\ \triangle OAB\)

    The triangle inside

    • \(\theta=60^\circ\): equilateral, \(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}r^2\)
    • \(\theta=90^\circ\): right-angled, \(\dfrac12r^2\)
    • \(\theta=120^\circ\): drop \(OM\perp AB\); the area is again \(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}r^2\)

    Clock hands

    • Minute hand: \(6^\circ\) per minute
    • Hour hand: \(30^\circ\) per hour
    • Area swept \(=\) sector of radius \(=\) length of hand
    • Tip travels the arc, not the area

    Tethered animals, vertex sectors

    • Corner of a square/rectangle \(\Rightarrow\) quadrant
    • Sectors at the 3 vertices of a triangle \(=\) semicircle
    • Sectors at the 4 corners of a quadrilateral \(=\) full circle
    • Ungrazed \(=\) field \(-\) grazed

    Working backwards

    • Area \(=\dfrac12\times\text{arc}\times r\) — the quickest link
    • Given arc or area \(\Rightarrow\) find \(\theta\) or \(r\)
    • Given perimeter \(\Rightarrow\) arc \(=\) perimeter \(-\,2r\)

    Formulas and facts to remember

    • Length of an arc\(\ell=\dfrac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r\)
    • Area of a sector\(A=\dfrac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2\)
    • Sector area from the arc\(A=\dfrac12\times\ell\times r\)
    • Perimeter of a sector\(P=\ell+2r\)
    • Area of a segment\(=\dfrac{\theta}{360}\pi r^2-\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)\)
    • Major sector / segment\(=\) area of the circle \(-\) the minor one
    • Triangle in the sector\(\text{ar}(\triangle OAB)=\dfrac12r^2\sin\theta\); for \(60^\circ\) it is \(\dfrac{\sqrt3}{4}r^2\) and for \(90^\circ\) it is \(\dfrac12r^2\)
    • Chord\(AB=2r\sin\dfrac{\theta}{2}\): it is \(r\) for \(60^\circ\), \(r\sqrt2\) for \(90^\circ\) and \(r\sqrt3\) for \(120^\circ\).
    • Take\(\pi=\dfrac{22}{7}\) unless the question says otherwise; \(\sqrt3=1.73\), \(\sqrt2=1.41\)

    Mistakes that cost marks

    • Writing \(\dfrac{\theta}{180}\times\pi r^2\) for the area of a sector.A whole turn is \(360^\circ\), so the fraction is always \(\dfrac{\theta}{360}\).
    • Giving only the arc as the “perimeter of the sector”.Perimeter \(=\) arc \(+\ 2r\). For a segment it is arc \(+\) chord.
    • Adding the triangle to the sector to get the segment.Minor segment \(=\) sector \(-\ \triangle OAB\). Sketch it once and you will never add again.
    • Taking \(1\) minute \(=1^\circ\) for the minute hand.\(360^\circ\) in 60 minutes, so \(6^\circ\) per minute. The hour hand turns \(30^\circ\) per hour.
    • Using the diameter in place of the radius.“Diameter 28 cm” means \(r=14\) cm. Write \(r=\ldots\) as the first line of every solution.
    • Thinking that doubling the rope doubles the grazing area.Area \(\propto r^2\), so it becomes four times. Only the arc doubles.
    • Letting the animal graze a full circle at the corner of a field.A corner is \(90^\circ\), so it is a quadrant — and check that the rope is shorter than the side.
    • Mixing units: writing cm for an area or cm\(^2\) for a length.Arc and perimeter in cm; sector and segment in cm\(^2\).
